Three-dimensional (3D) spatial perception from Light Detection and Ranging (LiDAR) point clouds serves as an indispensable sensory pillar for safe autonomous navigation, mobile robotics, and intelligent transportation systems. Unlike passive optical cameras, which remain vulnerable to ambient illumination fluctuations, adverse weather conditions, and projective scale ambiguities, LiDAR sensors actively emit pulsed laser beams that measure direct metric ranges, capturing high-precision geometric surface boundaries under arbitrary day-and-night conditions~\cite{geiger2012kitti}. In time-critical vehicular environments, perception systems must accurately detect, orient, and classify surrounding objects---spanning passenger vehicles to morphologically compact and vulnerable road users such as pedestrians and cyclists---within strict computational latency envelopes, typically requiring sub-$100\,\text{ms}$ execution per scan~\cite{yang2018pixor}. However, deploying high-accuracy 3D object detection models on embedded vehicular platforms faces an acute operational tension between detection accuracy and hardware resource consumption. Commodity onboard edge accelerators operate under stringent thermal and electrical power budgets, where memory bandwidth, cache capacity, and arithmetic throughput are strictly constrained. Consequently, developing ultra-compact yet highly discriminative LiDAR object detection architectures that sustain real-time execution without forfeiting spatial localization fidelity remains an essential prerequisite for scalable autonomous driving.

To extract 3D bounding boxes from unstructured point clouds, existing detection paradigms fall into three primary methodological families, each exhibiting inherent architectural trade-offs on resource-constrained platforms. First, volumetric voxel-based architectures, exemplified by SECOND~\cite{yan2018second}, and two-stage point-voxel frameworks, such as PointRCNN~\cite{shi2019pointrcnn}, Part-$A^2$~\cite{shi2020part}, and PV-RCNN~\cite{shi2020pvrcnn}, achieve competitive detection accuracy by deploying 3D sparse convolutions or localized point-cloud abstractions. Nevertheless, their multi-million parameter footprints ($4.0\text{--}63.8\,\text{M}$) and heavy memory access latencies ($100\text{--}195\,\text{ms}$) exceed the operational limits of embedded edge processors. Second, pillar-based detectors, pioneered by PointPillars~\cite{lang2019pointpillars} and modernized by PillarNeXt~\cite{li2023pillarnext} and PillarNeSt~\cite{mao2023pillarnest}, project points into vertical pillars processed via PointNet encoders to construct Bird's-Eye View (BEV) 2D feature representations. While faster than volumetric methods, non-deterministic point gathering, dynamic memory allocation, and sparse scattering introduce substantial runtime overhead and memory transfer latency. Third, proposal-free BEV rasterization methods, such as PIXOR~\cite{yang2018pixor} and MobilePIXOR~\cite{nguyen2024indoor}, eliminate learned encoders entirely by discretizing point clouds into fixed vertical occupancy grids. Although conceptually efficient, existing rasterization methods suffer from severe spatial quantization errors on standard $0.1\,\text{m}$ grids; by relying on crude binary occupancy slices, they discard continuous subcell offsets and high-order vertical dispersion statistics, inducing spatial staircasing and localization jitter on small objects. While recent studies such as PillarHist~\cite{zhou2025pillarhist} explore height histograms and TriBand-BEV~\cite{khoshkdahan2026triband} explores multi-band vertical slicing, they still omit continuous subcell displacements combined with comprehensive elevation moments. Furthermore, naive cut-and-paste data augmentation creates physical artifacts, small-scale backbones experience receptive-field starvation across disparate object dimensions, and uncalibrated classification heatmaps cause misaligned false positives to suppress well-localized candidates during Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS)~\cite{li2020gfl}. The failure of existing compact detectors to simultaneously resolve fine subcell quantization errors, unphysical augmentation artifacts, multi-scale receptive field coverage, and geometric score misalignment constitutes the core bottleneck impeding sub-million-parameter LiDAR perception.

This persistent bottleneck raises a fundamental research question: \textit{Can a compact LiDAR object detector operating strictly on a parameter-free 2D BEV representation match or exceed the accuracy of pillar-based and volumetric sparse-convolutional models while running substantially faster on commodity edge hardware?} Our central insight is that achieving state-of-the-art 3D detection does not strictly mandate parameter-heavy PointNet encoders or computationally demanding 3D convolutions. Instead, sub-voxel geometric precision can be preserved deterministically at the representation stage by coupling multi-band vertical densities and elevation dispersion moments with continuous subcell horizontal centroid displacements, entirely eliminating encoder latency and learned parameters. Furthermore, by pairing this enriched representation with a modern, single-branch ConvNeXt-style backbone~\cite{liu2022convnext} equipped with large $7 \times 7$ depthwise convolutions and parameter-free 3D spatial-energy attention (SimAM)~\cite{yang2021simam}, the network achieves expansive physical receptive fields that envelop full vehicles at early stages without dilation artifacts or multi-branch memory overhead. Finally, enforcing physical consistency during training data synthesis regularizes sparse returns, while multi-axis geometric supervision and localization-quality alignment directly synchronize backpropagation gradients and candidate ranking with continuous rotated bounding box geometry. Grounded in these insights, we demonstrate that a disciplined synergy of deterministic statistical rasterization, lightweight large-kernel convolution, energy-based attention, and geometric loss calibration can bridge the performance gap between compact 2D BEV detectors and multi-million-parameter 3D models.

Guided by this design philosophy, we propose \textbf{MobilePIXORNeXt}, an ultra-compact, proposal-free, multi-class 3D LiDAR object detector operating under a total budget of fewer than $0.76\,\text{M}$ trainable parameters. Figure~\ref{fig:r37_overview} illustrates the end-to-end data flow of our framework. An unordered LiDAR point cloud is deterministically transformed via the parameter-free \textbf{Strata-BEV14} rasterizer into a 14-channel statistical BEV tensor ($800 \times 704$ at $0.1\,\text{m}$ resolution) in under $21\,\text{ms}$, capturing height-band densities, vertical spatial moments ($z_{\max}, z_{\mathrm{mean}}, z_{\mathrm{span}}, z_{\mathrm{std}}$), surface reflectance statistics, and continuous subcell centroid displacement offsets $(\Delta\bar{x}, \Delta\bar{y})$ to eliminate quantization error. To regularize training without unphysical artifacts, we formulate \textbf{Physics-Consistent Ground-Truth Augmentation (PC-GTA)}, which validates candidate boxes via local ground elevation fitting, static obstacle clearance, line-of-sight occlusion checks, raycast LiDAR shadow masking, and range-adaptive point and intensity attenuation. Multi-scale feature extraction is performed by the \textbf{MobilePixorNeXt-S} backbone hierarchy across stages $C_2, C_3, C_4$, utilizing streamlined $7 \times 7$ depthwise inverted bottleneck blocks ($e=2.5$), SiLU activations, LayerScale stabilization, and embedded parameter-free \textbf{Block-SimAM} energy attention operating inside all eight residual blocks. Multi-scale semantic context and fine coordinates are balanced by the top-down \textbf{Scale-Gated Feature Pyramid Network (SG-FPN Neck, $F_{32}$)}, which employs bilinear upsampling, depthwise refinement, and learnable scale gates to eliminate deconvolution checkerboard artifacts. Predictions are decoupled into Car ($K_1=1$) and Pedestrian/Cyclist ($K_2=2$) categories via independent \textbf{GroupedHeader} towers sharing an initial $3 \times 3$ Conv-BN-SiLU block. During training, the network is supervised by \textbf{Oriented Geometric Alignment (OGA)}---coupling Multi-Axis Projection Generalized IoU (MGIoU)~\cite{le2026mgiou} with scale-normalized $\pi$-symmetric corner distance---and dynamically balanced via 12-parameter \textbf{Temperature-Softmax Bounded Uncertainty Weighting (T-SBUW)}. At inference, predicted boxes are ranked using composite scores from \textbf{IoU-Aware Quality Alignment (IQA)} ($s_{\mathrm{det}} = \sqrt{p_{\mathrm{cls}}\hat{q}}$) before class-wise rotated Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS). By deterministically capturing continuous geometry at the input, expanding receptive fields via single-branch large-kernel convolution, and supervising rotated overlap directly, this unified pipeline resolves each aforementioned bottleneck within a unified, edge-deployable framework.

The principal contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ultra-compact proposal-free BEV detection.} We develop \textbf{MobilePIXORNeXt}, an ultra-compact ($753{,}569$ trainable parameters) LiDAR detector that eliminates learned PointNet encoders and 3D sparse convolutions. The architecture integrates the deterministic 14-channel \textbf{Strata-BEV14} representation, the streamlined \textbf{MobilePixorNeXt-S} backbone hierarchy with embedded parameter-free \textbf{Block-SimAM} energy attention, the \textbf{SG-FPN} neck ($F_{32}$), and decoupled \textbf{GroupedHeader} towers to balance multi-scale road categories.
    
    \item \textbf{Physics-consistent data augmentation and oriented geometric supervision.} We formulate \textbf{PC-GTA}, which enforces local terrain alignment, obstacle clearance, raycast shadow masking, and range-dependent attenuation, eliminating unphysical synthesis artifacts. Furthermore, we develop the \textbf{OGA} loss strategy, combining Multi-Axis Projection GIoU with scale-normalized $\pi$-symmetric corner supervision, dynamically balanced across multi-task objectives via 12-parameter \textbf{T-SBUW} and coupled with \textbf{IQA} localization-quality confidence calibration.
    
    \item \textbf{Extensive validation and state-of-the-art edge Pareto efficiency.} Benchmarked on the official KITTI validation dataset ($R_{40}$), MobilePIXORNeXt establishes a leading accuracy-to-parameter and accuracy-to-latency trade-off. It achieves \textbf{71.46\% Moderate mAP} and \textbf{73.42\% $\text{mAP}_9$} with only \textbf{0.754\,M parameters} ($9.34\,\text{MB}$ checkpoint), surpassing PointPillars ($70.13\%$) with an \textbf{84.4\% parameter reduction} and closely trailing SECOND ($72.09\%$) with \textbf{85.8\% fewer parameters}. In complete end-to-end pipeline speed, it operates at \textbf{16.41\,FPS} (\textbf{60.96\,ms} on NVIDIA Tesla T4), running faster than all evaluated real-time detectors. On vulnerable road users, it attains \textbf{60.75\% Moderate AP} on Pedestrian (+4.74\% over PointPillars, +4.19\% over SECOND). Controlled ablations demonstrate that PC-GTA yields a decisive \textbf{+6.03\% Moderate mAP} gain ($60.22\%$ to $66.25\%$), OGA and IQA deliver a \textbf{+3.20\% mAP} surge, and Block-SimAM provides an additional \textbf{+0.88\% mAP} at zero parameter overhead.
\end{itemize}
