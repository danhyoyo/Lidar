LiDAR object detection provides geometric information for autonomous navigation by estimating the locations, dimensions, and orientations of surrounding objects. Road scenes contain objects with markedly different spatial extents, while range and occlusion affect how much evidence is available for each instance. Benchmarks such as KITTI make these differences visible across object classes and difficulty levels~\cite{geiger2012kitti}. For a compact detector, the resulting challenge is to preserve useful geometric detail while limiting the cost of encoding, feature extraction, and prediction. Network size alone does not describe this cost: point-cloud preprocessing and decoding also contribute to the time required to produce detections.

Bird's-eye-view (BEV) detection offers a practical way to use two-dimensional convolutional networks on LiDAR data. PIXOR demonstrated dense, proposal-free prediction from a BEV representation~\cite{yang2018pixor}. Nguyen et al.\ subsequently developed IRBGHR-PIXOR, combining inverted residual blocks and Gaussian heatmap regression for indoor pedestrian detection~\cite{nguyen2024indoor}; this work provides the baseline lineage referred to as MobilePIXOR in the present project. Tran et al.\ extended MobilePIXOR to rubber-tree detection through attention configuration and teacher--student distillation~\cite{tran2026rubber}. These studies establish a compact detector family, while motivating further investigation of representation, multiscale features, and supervision for multiclass road-scene detection.

Transferring image-network components to LiDAR is already an established research direction. PillarNeXt investigates computational allocation and receptive-field design~\cite{li2023pillarnext}, and PillarNeSt adapts ConvNeXt-based backbones to pillar representations~\cite{mao2023pillarnest}. Accordingly, the research question here concerns how a particular combination of input statistics, feature processing, and training objectives behaves under a compact model budget. This question is especially relevant when a common feature hierarchy must support both large vehicle footprints and substantially smaller pedestrian and cyclist footprints.

We develop MobilePIXORNeXt around a deterministic fourteen-channel BEV representation, termed Strata-BEV14, drawing on height-stratified BEV encoding in TriBand-BEV~\cite{khoshkdahan2026triband} and the height-histogram principle of PillarHist~\cite{zhou2025pillarhist}. Its channels encode height distributions and statistics, reflectance, occupancy, density, and horizontal offsets within each cell. A narrow backbone with large depthwise kernels processes this representation, while scale-gated fusion combines features at different resolutions. Parameter-free SimAM refines the residual updates inside the backbone blocks at all three feature scales. Grouped prediction heads share an initial convolution across prediction tasks within each group and accommodate the three target classes in the main BEV detector. These choices define a specific LiDAR architecture whose individual components and interactions can be examined.

Training must also account for the relationship between coordinate errors and the geometry of the decoded box. We formulate Oriented Geometric Alignment (OGA) by combining coordinate supervision with a projection-based overlap objective derived from MGIoU~\cite{le2026mgiou} and a scale-normalized, orientation-symmetric corner term. Running loss-scale normalization and bounded adaptive task weights combine the training terms. Localization quality is considered through an optional quality prediction branch and a quality-coupled loss alternative, Q-OGA, motivated by prior work on joint classification and localization-quality learning~\cite{li2020gfl}. This formulation allows box geometry and confidence ranking to be investigated within the same compact detector family.

The principal contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{A compact detector for multiclass LiDAR perception.}
    We develop MobilePIXORNeXt by integrating a deterministic fourteen-channel BEV representation, large-kernel depthwise feature processing, scale-gated multiscale fusion, and grouped prediction heads with oriented geometric training. The final configuration applies SimAM within the blocks at all three backbone stages, uses a 32-channel final fusion layer, and shares the first head convolution across tasks within each class group.

    \item \textbf{An empirical study of geometric supervision and localization-quality prediction.}
    We examine the interaction between OGA and a separate IQA branch through a $2\times2$ factorial comparison, and compare OGA with Q-OGA without a separate quality head, with matched training conditions required for attribution. These experiments assess whether geometric training and explicit quality prediction provide complementary or overlapping benefits, while distinguishing the effects of the complete training formulations from those of individual loss terms.

    \item \textbf{An evaluation of compactness and inference cost.}
    We assess detection quality alongside parameter count, model-forward latency, and complete input-to-detections latency. Separating these quantities exposes the cost of preprocessing and postprocessing and avoids treating model FPS as pipeline throughput. Component comparisons examine representation, fusion width, and attention placement under their recorded configurations.
\end{itemize}

The study focuses on BEV footprints for Car, Pedestrian, and Cyclist. Ablation and detector-comparison measurements are kept as separate experiment sets until their evaluation and timing conditions are reconciled. The current evidence supports a compact-model study; claims about statistical significance, embedded deployment, and learned vertical localization require additional experiments.
