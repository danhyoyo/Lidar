\subsection{Interaction between geometry and quality}
Table~\ref{tab:factorial} shows the 50-epoch OGA--IQA comparison with the same Hist14 backbone, focal context, grouped heads, augmentation, and decoding thresholds. Without IQA, OGA raises Moderate mAP from 67.38\% to 68.97\%, a gain of 1.59 percentage points (pp). With IQA, its gain is 0.27 pp. Conversely, IQA adds 2.93 pp to the baseline objective and 1.61 pp to OGA. The difference in these gains is $-1.32$ pp, consistent with overlapping benefits in this seed (Fig.~\ref{fig:tradeoff}a). The combination has the highest mean among these four runs, although Cyclist AP is slightly lower than for baseline plus IQA. This comparison does not isolate OGA's projection, corner, and weighting components.

\subsection{Matched OGA--Q-OGA retraining}
With the same 100-epoch SimAM configuration and no separate IQA branch, Q-OGA raises Moderate mAP from OGA's 69.69\% to 70.52\% (Table~\ref{tab:loss_retrain}). Both runs have 815,744 detector parameters and select epoch 70 using the same scheduled AP criterion. Their supplied configurations differ only in the objective name. The 0.82 pp macro-average gain comprises a 4.21 pp Pedestrian increase, a 0.11 pp Car reduction, and a 1.62 pp Cyclist reduction. Thus, the improvement is concentrated in Pedestrian detection rather than shared across all three classes.

Q-OGA scores higher at nine of the ten scheduled AP evaluations, with the exception at epoch 20 (Fig.~\ref{fig:loss_retrain}). At epoch 100, it scores 70.32\% versus OGA's 68.79\%, a 1.53 pp difference. The separately loss-selected checkpoints yield 69.80\% and 69.69\%, respectively, reducing the advantage to 0.11 pp. The reported primary comparison therefore depends on the declared checkpoint criterion. These two runs extend the training budget but retain seed 42; they do not supply independent-seed replication.

At score threshold 0.05, Q-OGA produces 20.62 false positives per frame versus OGA's 31.52, while micro recall is 89.67\% versus 89.49\%. Total false positives fall from 118,795 to 77,704 (34.59\%). These fixed-threshold operating points do not establish equal-recall superiority or calibrated probabilities. Model forward latency is similar, 7.93 versus 7.88 ms, while the saved offline pipeline time decreases from 47.32 to 42.10 ms. The largest stage reduction is decoding/suppression, from 21.05 to 15.53 ms, consistent with the reduced candidate output. This is an observed pipeline difference for the same detector architecture.

\subsection{Context, attention, and earlier design choices}
Removing focal context from OGA plus IQA lowers mAP from 70.58\% (P4) to 68.72\% (P5). However, adding it to the baseline configuration lowers the score from 68.46\% (S32) to 67.38\% (P1). Context therefore has a conditional effect within the available configurations. Among local refinement variants (Table~\ref{tab:attention}), SimAM yields the highest mAP, 70.99\%. Its 0.41 pp gain over P4 includes a 1.03 pp Pedestrian improvement and a small Car reduction. The detail path and ECA produce lower macro-averages despite small Pedestrian increases.

The earlier runs provide supporting design evidence rather than a fully controlled sequence. Hist14 scores 50.14\% compared with 47.37\% for Rich8, and grouped heads score 51.30\%, but these runs also differ in code or configuration details. A more closely aligned width comparison raises mAP from 66.56\% to 67.28\% when prediction width increases from 16 to 32 at fusion width 24, adding 114,832 parameters. Changing fusion width from 24 to 32 at prediction width 16 lowers mAP by 0.31 pp. The apparent 7.91 pp sampling gain between O32 and H32 accompanies simultaneous changes to sampling probability, quotas, placement attempts, and validation batching. Supplementary Tables~S2--S5 retain all runs and identify these comparison limits.

\subsection{Range performance and runtime}
P16's Pedestrian AP in the 30--50 m bin rises from P4's 4.54\% to 9.77\%, with 71 rather than 66 true positives among 137 valid objects (Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}b). The absolute score remains low. The 50--70.4 m bin contains only 13 Pedestrians, limiting interpretation of its score. These bins expose remaining range sensitivity rather than establish robust distant-object detection.

P16 uses 852,930 detector parameters and averages 8.55 ms per model forward pass, equivalent to 116.90 forwards/s. The offline input-to-detections pipeline averages 56.83 ms, or 17.60 frames/s, with a 95th percentile of 97.37 ms. Its loading/encoding, transfer, model, and decoding means are 11.74, 6.75, 8.55, and 29.51 ms. Decoding is the largest recorded stage (Fig.~\ref{fig:latency}a). P3 has a higher model time than P4 but a shorter total pipeline time, illustrating the effect of the number and distribution of decoded candidates. No embedded-device throughput is established by these L4 measurements.

\subsection{Reference evaluation of the trained 3D extension}
Final3D achieves 71.09\% BEV and 57.75\% 3D Moderate mAP under the pinned reference evaluator (Table~\ref{tab:reference}). The detector has 797,382 parameters: 666,769 in the backbone including the neck, and 130,613 in the grouped heads. This total is 55,548 parameters below the archived independent-head P16 BEV model, despite the added vertical outputs. The models differ in head topology, training duration, checkpoint criterion, and evaluation protocol, so their AP difference is not a controlled shared-head ablation. Final3D is selected at epoch 70; its epoch-100 3D score is 56.47\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:vertical}a). The loss-selected epoch-24 checkpoint scores 46.73\%, reinforcing the practical relevance of the declared selection policy.

\subsection{Vertical localization and qualitative evidence}
Among 7,130 fixed BEV-matched Cars in the CPU diagnostic, 5,743 pass the 3D IoU threshold of 0.7. Replacing vertical bottom location raises the count to 6,791, whereas replacing height alone gives 5,873. Joint replacement makes all 7,130 fixed pairs pass (Fig.~\ref{fig:vertical}b). Vertical location correction recovers 1,048 of the 1,387 failing Car pairs, or 75.6\%. Car bottom-height mean absolute error is 8.66 cm, while signed bias is only $-0.82$ cm. The error is thus substantially object-dependent. Car pass rates decrease from 91.46\% within 20 m to 53.29\% beyond 40 m among the matched pairs. These conditional counts identify a vertical localization bottleneck without predicting the AP gain of an oracle system.
