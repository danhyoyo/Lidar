\subsection{Dataset and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:dataset_and_metrics}

\paragraph{Dataset and Partitioning.}
We evaluated the detector on the KITTI 3D Object Detection dataset~\cite{geiger2012kitti}. The dataset contains $7{,}481$ calibrated LiDAR scans with synchronized rectified camera images and 3D bounding box annotations from road scenes. We used the 50/50 split introduced by Chen et al.~\cite{yang2018pixor}, with $3{,}712$ training frames and $3{,}769$ validation frames. Ground-truth sampling databases were generated exclusively from the $3{,}712$ training frames to prevent validation data leakage.

\paragraph{Spatial Region of Interest (ROI) and Target Parameterization.}
Following Section~\ref{sec:problem_formulation}, we aligned the LiDAR coordinate frame with the vehicle body (forward $X$, leftward $Y$, upward $Z$). We restricted perception to the Cartesian Region of Interest:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        x &\in [0.0, 70.4]\,\text{m}, \\
        y &\in [-40.0, 40.0]\,\text{m}, \\
        z &\in [-2.5, 1.0]\,\text{m}.
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We applied an open-boundary inward margin of $\epsilon = 0.001\,\text{m}$ along all three axes. A horizontal grid resolution of $\Delta_x = \Delta_y = 0.1\,\text{m}$ produced an input tensor of $H_{\mathrm{in}} \times W_{\mathrm{in}} = 800 \times 704$ cells. An output stride of $s_{\mathrm{out}} = 4$ gave feature maps of size $H_{\mathrm{out}} \times W_{\mathrm{out}} = 200 \times 176$. We evaluated Car, Pedestrian, and Cyclist detections.

\paragraph{Evaluation Metrics.}
We evaluated oriented 2D Bird's-Eye View (BEV) footprints on the KITTI validation set using 40-point interpolated Average Precision (AP $R_{40}$). This metric averages interpolated precision at 40 recall positions $\mathcal{R}_{40} = \{1/40, 2/40, \dots, 1.0\}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{AP}|_{R_{40}} = \frac{1}{40} \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}_{40}} \max_{r' \ge r} p(r').
\end{equation}
Following KITTI matching conventions, we set oriented bounding box Intersection-over-Union (IoU) thresholds to $\tau_{\mathrm{IoU}} = 0.70$ for Car and $\tau_{\mathrm{IoU}} = 0.50$ for Pedestrian and Cyclist. We grouped detections into three difficulty regimes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Easy}: 2D bounding box height $\ge 40\,\text{px}$, maximum occlusion level = 0 (fully visible), maximum truncation $\le 15\%$.
    \item \textbf{Moderate}: 2D bounding box height $\ge 25\,\text{px}$, maximum occlusion level = 1 (partially occluded), maximum truncation $\le 30\%$.
    \item \textbf{Hard}: 2D bounding box height $\ge 25\,\text{px}$, maximum occlusion level = 2 (difficult to see), maximum truncation $\le 50\%$.
\end{itemize}
Following autonomous driving literature, \textbf{Moderate macro mAP} ($\mathrm{mAP}_{\mathrm{M}}$) was adopted as the primary benchmark ranking criterion:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{mAP}_{\mathrm{M}} = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{c \in \{\mathrm{Car}, \mathrm{Ped}, \mathrm{Cyc}\}} \mathrm{AP}_{c, \mathrm{M}}.
\end{equation}
We also reported the unweighted mean across all nine class-difficulty combinations:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{mAP}_{9} = \frac{1}{9} \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} \sum_{d \in \{\mathrm{E}, \mathrm{M}, \mathrm{H}\}} \mathrm{AP}_{c, d}.
\end{equation}
MobilePIXORNeXt and historical MobilePIXOR results were computed with the local loader-aligned BEV evaluator. Reference detector results in Table~\ref{tab:comparison} used the reference KITTI evaluator. Both implementations used $R_{40}$ interpolation on the same validation split and are identified separately in the table. E, M, and H denote Easy, Moderate, and Hard. $\mathrm{mAP}_{9}$ is the unweighted average across the 9-cell evaluation grid, rather than AP at an IoU threshold of 0.9.

\paragraph{Network and Model Configuration.}
MobilePIXORNeXt processes 14-channel Strata-BEV14 tensors. We used a two-layer $3 \times 3$ convolutional stem ($32$ channels, stride 2) and three backbone stages with channel widths $(48, 96, 128)$ and block depths $(3, 4, 2)$. The stages used \texttt{MobilePIXORNeXt-S} blocks with $7 \times 7$ depthwise convolutions, an inverted expansion ratio of $e=2.5$, SiLU activations, and LayerScale initialized to $10^{-5}$. Parameter-free Block-SimAM attention was embedded in all nine residual blocks. The Scale-Gated Feature Pyramid Network (SG-FPN) fused stages C4, C3, and C2 into a $F_{32}$ (32-channel) representation at stride 4 ($200 \times 176$). Decoupled GroupedHeader towers processed Car ($K_1=1$) and Pedestrian/Cyclist ($K_2=2$) independently, with an initial $3 \times 3$ Conv-BN-SiLU transformation per group. The detector contained $753{,}569$ trainable parameters, with an FP32 checkpoint size of $9.34\,\text{MB}$.

\paragraph{Training Schedule and Optimization.}
We optimized the model with AdamW using fused CUDA kernels. The base learning rate was $\eta_0 = 7 \times 10^{-4}$, with a minimum of $\eta_{\min} = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}$. Weight decay of $\lambda = 0.001$ applied only to multidimensional weight matrices ($\ge 2\mathrm{D}$ tensors). Biases, Batch Normalization affine parameters, and task-uncertainty logits were exempt ($\lambda = 0.0$). Gradients were clipped to a maximum $L_2$ norm of $10.0$. The standard schedule covered 50 epochs, ending at epoch 50, after 4 linear warmup epochs and 46 cosine annealing epochs down to $\eta_{\min}$. The reported checkpoint came from an extended 160-epoch run with 10 warmup epochs and was selected at epoch 130. Section~\ref{sec:ablations} validates schedule fairness by comparing this checkpoint against the 50-epoch minimum validation loss checkpoint. Temperature-Softmax Bounded Uncertainty Weighting (T-SBUW, Section~\ref{sec:losses_and_oga}) balanced the task losses. Section~\ref{sec:ablations} evaluates its empirical convergence behavior.

\paragraph{Data Augmentation Pipeline.}
Training used Physics-Consistent Ground-Truth Augmentation (\texttt{PC-GTA}; Section~\ref{sec:pc_gta}). The final reported run enabled local ground fitting, static obstacle clearance, range-adaptive point subsampling, and visibility protection. The static obstacle clearance threshold was set to $N_{\mathrm{thresh}} = 2$ points within the expanded footprint, and estimated ground elevation was bounded within $[-2.5, -0.8]\,\text{m}$. LOS rejection, shadow masking, and radiometric calibration were available as configurable options but disabled in the primary benchmark. Ground height was estimated within $R(d) = \operatorname{clip}(2.5 + 0.03\max(0, d - 15), 2.5, 4.0)\,\text{m}$. GT sampling was applied with probability $0.5$. Subsequent world transforms included flipping along the $X$-axis with probability $0.5$, rotation $\theta \sim \mathcal{U}(-45^\circ,45^\circ)$, and isotropic scaling $s \sim \mathcal{U}(0.95,1.05)$. World translation was applied with probability $0.5$, using independent Gaussian noise with standard deviations $(0.2,0.2,0.1)\,\text{m}$ along $(X,Y,Z)$.

\paragraph{Batching, Precision, and Hardware Environment.}
Training used a physical batch size of 16 on a single GPU; validation used a batch size of 32. DataLoader processes used 6 persistent worker threads, pinned memory, and a prefetch factor of 2. Forward and backward passes used Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP) in Bfloat16 (\texttt{bf16}). Loss evaluation, coordinate regression, and rotated NMS used full FP32 precision for numerical stability. Final MobilePIXORNeXt timing and accuracy evaluation used an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU (16\,GB GDDR6 VRAM, Turing architecture), an x86\_64 host CPU, Linux OS, and PyTorch 2.11.0 with CUDA 13.0. Peak allocated evaluation GPU memory was $98.44\,\text{MiB}$ ($103.22\,\text{MB}$ decimal). Historical MobilePIXOR timing was recorded on an NVIDIA L4 and is identified separately in Table~\ref{tab:efficiency}. All KITTI ablation and comparison metrics came from deterministic single-seed evaluation ($\text{seed} = 42$) on the same validation split. Variance and standard deviation ($\pm\,\text{std}$) across seeds were left for future multi-fold evaluation. Table~\ref{tab:setup} summarizes the implementation settings.
