\section{Discussion}
The strongest internal evidence is the geometry--quality interaction. Both OGA and IQA improve the baseline when used individually, while the combined gain is smaller than their sum. This pattern is consistent with partial overlap between improved geometric learning and localization-aware ranking. Because IQA's target is derived from the same projection similarity used in OGA, a shared geometric signal is a plausible explanation. The experiment does not establish that explanation causally, and replication across seeds is required to assess the stability of the observed interaction.

The matched 100-epoch comparison supports a modest macro-average benefit for coupling quality to the classification objective without adding an IQA branch. Its 0.82 pp AP-selected gain is driven by Pedestrian, with lower Car and Cyclist AP, and decreases to 0.11 pp under loss-based selection. Q-OGA changes quality focal supervision, corner assignment, and range weighting together, so their individual contributions remain unresolved. The two retrained runs share seed 42 and a larger budget than the factorial study; they strengthen the direct objective comparison without measuring the interaction between Q-OGA and IQA.

Compactness also involves choices beyond the backbone. The trained shared-head model remains below 0.8 million detector parameters while adding vertical regression, but the saved efficiency evidence covers the independent-head BEV models. Their runtime decomposition indicates that decoding and candidate volume can outweigh feature-processing costs. A deployment study should therefore measure loading, transfer, suppression, and output delivery under a fixed hardware and precision setting, rather than infer complete-system speed from model forward frequency.

The 3D results expose a mismatch between footprint-based confidence and cuboid accuracy. IQA supervises horizontal geometry, and inference suppresses detections in BEV. Neither mechanism explicitly rewards vertical overlap. The conditional diagnostic suggests that object-dependent vertical location errors are a useful target for future development, especially for distant Cars. A three-dimensional quality target, vertical-aware training, or a ground-conditioned representation should be evaluated through fresh matched experiments; the current oracle counts are not estimates of their attainable AP.

Several limitations bound the conclusions. The study uses one dataset, one random seed, and a validation set also used for checkpoint and variant selection. It lacks a hidden-test result, independent contemporary baselines under the reference evaluator, and a matched shared-versus-independent 3D study. Some historical comparisons change multiple variables. The local and reference protocols have different ground-truth domains, and the CPU diagnostic differs from both AP implementations. The work consequently establishes a compact implementation and useful within-project measurements, while leaving claims of superiority over modern detectors and embedded deployment performance to further evaluation.
