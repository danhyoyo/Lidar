\subsection{BEV representations and efficient LiDAR detection}

A BEV representation maps irregular point-cloud observations onto a regular horizontal grid. PIXOR uses this structure for dense oriented-box prediction with a fully convolutional detector~\cite{yang2018pixor}. PointPillars instead learns features from points grouped into vertical columns and scatters the resulting features into a pseudo-image for two-dimensional processing~\cite{lang2019pointpillars}. These approaches illustrate different choices about where representation learning occurs: in the point-to-grid encoder, in the subsequent convolutional network, or in both.

PillarNeXt revisits the allocation of computation between local point aggregation and the detection network. Its results show that simple pillar representations can benefit substantially from changes to receptive fields, architecture, and training~\cite{li2023pillarnext}. This motivates examining representation and backbone design together rather than assuming that a more elaborate encoder is always preferable. In the present work, Strata-BEV14 fixes the point-to-grid transformation and supplies explicit height, reflectance, density, occupancy, and within-cell statistics. Its utility is therefore assessed through matched encoding comparisons, while the learned detector operates on the resulting BEV tensor.

TriBand-BEV retains coarse vertical structure using three reflectance channels, one per height band~\cite{khoshkdahan2026triband}. This motivates the use of height strata in our deterministic encoding. Strata-BEV8 and Strata-BEV14 employ occupancy or count bands with additional cell statistics; they do not reuse the original three-channel tensor. Our study evaluates these encodings within the MobilePIXORNeXt family, without attributing the general principle of height-stratified BEV to this work.

PillarHist encodes height-bin counts and mean reflectance, combines them with pillar-center coordinates, and learns a pillar-level projection~\cite{zhou2025pillarhist}. Strata-BEV14 adapts the height-count idea into a fixed dense raster with four log-count bands and ten cell-statistic channels. It has no learned pillar projection or per-band reflectance channels.

\subsection{MobilePIXOR and task-specific adaptations}


The closest baseline is the indoor pedestrian detector of Nguyen et al.~\cite{nguyen2024indoor}. Their published method, IRBGHR-PIXOR, remodels the PIXOR backbone using inverted residual blocks, predicts pedestrian centers through Gaussian heatmap regression, and employs a modified focal objective. Evaluation on JRDB addresses perception for mobile robots operating around people. We use the term MobilePIXOR for the corresponding lightweight baseline family in this study, while retaining the original method name when discussing the cited publication.

Tran et al.~\cite{tran2026rubber} investigate MobilePIXOR for rubber-tree perception using a geometry-only BEV occupancy representation. Their study examines multiscale attention and spatial/channel squeeze-and-excitation configurations and uses knowledge distillation to obtain smaller student models. This is a direct precedent for task-specific adaptation and systematic configuration of a compact LiDAR detector. The present work studies multiclass KITTI detection with statistical BEV encoding and geometry- and quality-aware supervision. The different datasets, object classes, and evaluation procedures require separate numerical comparisons.

\subsection{Modern convolutional backbones and feature refinement}

ConvNeXt revisits convolutional network design using, among other elements, large depthwise kernels and channel transformations~\cite{liu2022convnext}. PillarNeSt explicitly transfers ConvNeXt-based design to LiDAR pillar detectors and studies backbone scaling and image-based pretraining~\cite{mao2023pillarnest}. Thus, the use of a modern image-derived backbone in a point-cloud detector has clear prior art. MobilePIXORNeXt uses a compact configuration of related convolutional principles with deterministic BEV input and scale-gated multiscale fusion; the contribution lies in that design and its evaluation rather than in originating large-kernel depthwise convolution.

SimAM derives feature-map attention weights without adding parameters to its original attention operator~\cite{yang2021simam}. We use SimAM as the feature-refinement module in our detector. The selected architecture applies it inside the residual blocks at all three backbone stages, after projection Batchnorm and before the existing LayerScale. This placement adds no learned parameters. A stage-level residual adapter introduces a separate learned scale, so these placements are distinguished in the ablation study. SimAM is attributed to its original source and assessed as a component of the detector.

\subsection{Oriented geometry, task balancing, and localization quality}

Coordinate regression provides direct supervision of box parameters, but parameter errors do not uniquely determine overlap between oriented boxes. Generalized Intersection over Union (GIoU) adds an enclosing-region penalty to IoU, providing a geometric signal even for separated boxes~\cite{rezatofighi2019giou}. Marginalized Generalized Intersection over Union (MGIoU) compares convex shapes through projections onto their defining directions and aggregates one-dimensional GIoU over these axes~\cite{le2026mgiou}. OGA applies these projections to BEV footprints and adds a corner discrepancy normalized by target size. The corner correspondence respects the $\pi$ symmetry of the detector's doubled-angle representation. The resulting objective concerns footprint geometry; vertical location and height are outside the output space of the main model studied here.

Combining classification and geometric losses also requires a choice of relative scale. Kendall et al.\ formulate multi-task weighting using task uncertainty~\cite{kendall2018uncertainty}. The balancing mechanism used here instead normalizes losses using running magnitudes and obtains positive task weights from bounded logits and a temperature-controlled softmax. It is treated as an adaptive optimization mechanism; its learned weights are not interpreted as calibrated uncertainty estimates.

Localization quality introduces a related distinction between recognizing an object and accurately fitting its box. Generalized Focal Loss combines classification and localization quality through continuous targets~\cite{li2020gfl}. Our detector supports a separate IoU-aware quality (IQA) branch for score fusion, while Q-OGA couples quality supervision to Gaussian center classification. The training target can be a projection-derived similarity or an exact rotated BEV overlap, depending on the configured variant; those quantities have different meanings and must be identified explicitly. These alternatives provide a way to study how geometric supervision and score ranking interact, without treating quality-aware scoring itself as a newly introduced principle.
