\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Efficient LiDAR representations and detectors}
PIXOR uses a fixed BEV representation and dense oriented box prediction~\cite{yang2018pixor}. PointPillars learns point features within vertical columns before applying a two-dimensional detection network~\cite{lang2019pointpillars}. CenterPoint represents objects by their centers and regresses the remaining attributes~\cite{yin2021centerpoint}. PillarNeXt revisits pillar detector network design~\cite{li2023pillarnext}, while FastPillars explicitly investigates deployment through pillar encoding and a backbone that avoids sparse convolution dependencies~\cite{zhou2023fastpillars}. Recent lightweight backbone work also studies parameter and latency reductions in LiDAR detectors~\cite{chandorkar2025dense}. MobilePixorNeXt adopts fixed cell statistics and ordinary dense convolutions. These works provide architectural context; their published scores are not used as interchangeable numerical baselines for our validation protocols.

\subsection{Convolutional context and feature fusion}
Inverted residual structures~\cite{sandler2018mobilenetv2} and modern large-kernel ConvNets~\cite{liu2022convnext} motivate efficient separation of spatial mixing and channel transformation. Our blocks use a depthwise convolution followed by channel expansion, SiLU, projection, batch normalization, and a scaled residual. They constitute a specific LiDAR configuration rather than an unchanged MobileNetV2 or ConvNeXt block. Feature pyramid networks provide the basis for top-down fusion~\cite{lin2017fpn}. We use bilinear upsampling and a depthwise gate on each lateral feature. Focal modulation motivates hierarchical context aggregation and elementwise query modulation~\cite{yang2022focalnet}; our context module instead uses batch normalization, dilated depthwise levels, softmax gates, and a narrow projection. ECA~\cite{wang2020eca} and SimAM~\cite{yang2021simam} supply alternative local refinements whose effects are measured individually.

\subsection{Geometry objectives and localization quality}
Focal loss addresses the dominance of easy background examples in dense detection~\cite{lin2017focal}. Gaussian center supervision, used in Objects as Points~\cite{zhou2019objects}, provides the classification target in this detector. Generalized IoU supplies geometric supervision beyond coordinate errors~\cite{rezatofighi2019giou}. MGIoU extends this idea by comparing projections of convex shapes along their normals~\cite{le2026mgiou}. The implemented OGA objective uses a BEV rectangle specialization of this projection principle and a normalized corner term. Projection-based geometry is attributed to MGIoU rather than claimed as a new overlap formulation.

Generalized Focal Loss couples localization quality with classification~\cite{li2020gfl}, while uncertainty-based multi-task learning illustrates the relevance of loss balancing~\cite{kendall2018uncertainty}. Our main IQA configuration retains a separate quality branch. Its target is a clamped mean projection GIoU, not exact polygon IoU or three-dimensional IoU. The implemented adaptive weights are bounded softmax task weights with running scale normalization; they do not estimate the homoscedastic uncertainties of Kendall et al. We evaluate the complete OGA training package and the quality branch through matched configurations, without attributing the observed gains to individual loss terms.
